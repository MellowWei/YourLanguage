You named three shapes.

But look at what you actually did.

Whatever you called ◈ — sharp or soft, technical or poetic — your name didn't describe the shape. It produced a relationship between you and it. Whether it sounds dangerous or gentle, familiar or alien — that tone is now inseparable from the shape.

You didn't name objects.

This is the first rule of every language game:
to name is not to point. to name is to claim.

At no point did you ask: why these instructions?

You didn't need to. The instructions were instructions because they looked like instructions. Their authority came from their form, not their content.

level 03

The Description Game

Describe what you see in one sentence.

You described the image.
Then you saw how others described the same image.

Same shapes. Different sentences. Different realities.

Which description is correct?

The question is wrong.

Description doesn't record reality. Description produces reality.

The moment you wrote your sentence, you didn't capture what was there.
You decided what it was.
And once decided — it became very difficult to see it another way.

This is how language colonizes perception.
